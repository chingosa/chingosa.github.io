# Research

I am a PhD researcher in climate dynamics at the University of St Andrews. My research investigates how physical processes within the climate system determine regional climate change, with a particular focus on extreme temperatures, land–atmosphere interactions, and tropical hydroclimate.

---

## Tropical land drying and humidity constraints

Climate models consistently project drying of tropical land regions, but the magnitude of this response varies substantially between models. My research investigates the physical mechanisms controlling this uncertainty and develops theory-based constraints on future changes in land humidity.

Key questions:

- How does land humidity respond to ocean warming?
- What controls the spread of projections across climate models?
- Can physical theory improve predictions of future hydroclimate change?

Methods include:

- Climate model analysis (CMIP6, AMIP experiments)
- Observational evaluation
- Moisture budget analysis
- Emergent constraints

---

## Extreme temperatures and climate sensitivity

Extreme temperatures are strongly influenced by land surface processes, atmospheric moisture, and radiative feedbacks. I study how these processes interact to shape changes in temperature extremes under global warming.

Research areas include:

- Land amplification of warming
- Humidity feedbacks
- Moist static energy constraints
- Surface energy balance

---

## Atmospheric dynamics and moist static energy

The atmospheric circulation redistributes energy and moisture around the planet. My work explores how constraints based on moist static energy and atmospheric dynamics can explain regional climate responses.

Current interests include:

- Weak Temperature Gradient (WTG) theory
- Convective quasi-equilibrium
- Walker circulation changes
- Atmospheric energy transport

---

## Climate modelling

I use numerical climate models and large ensembles to understand the mechanisms driving climate variability and change.

Tools and datasets:

- CMIP6 climate models
- AMIP experiments
- Reanalysis products
- Idealised climate models
