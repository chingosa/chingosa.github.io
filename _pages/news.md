Recent events/happenings!

---
**September 2026:**

We had our second annual Global-Ex away day in St Andrews, where we heard about the group’s plans moving forward over the next year. It was really fun reflecting on what everyone has accomplished this year and hearing about the ambitious plans people have for the year ahead. I’m particularly excited to spend the next few months exploring the role of patterned warming in mediating the land–ocean warming contrast over the historical period.

We also had the pleasure of hosting Koh Kawaguchi from Imperial College London in St Andrews for a few days. Koh presented his work on Earth’s energy imbalance, which led to some really interesting conversations. We had some great chats about science, research, and everything in between!

**June 2026:**

I attended the Land Climate Conference hosted by St Andrews, which brought an international crowd of scientists right to my doorstep (how convenient!). It was fantastic to hear about the state of the art in land–climate research, and a real masterclass in presentation giving from senior academics. I also had the opportunity to present my own work in poster form, where I received lots of great feedback and had some really interesting discussions over the world cup matches that were happening concurrently. I hope to attend many more of these in the future!

**May 2026:**

I attended EGU2026 and presented a talk entitled [Amplified future drying of tropical land constrained by physical theory](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-5957.html) in the CL4.4 session on land–atmosphere interactions and climate extremes where Mike also presented some [other work](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-1932.html) that I am involeved in. It was an exhausting but thoroughly invigorating week that left me really excited about the field. I particularly enjoyed how, even at a conference of more than 20,000 people, you still end up meeting and talking to people working in your specific area, while also getting the chance to catch up with people you've met at other conferences and events. It was a great reminder of just how broad the climate community is, while still feeling surprisingly connected.

Additionally I passed my second year annual review this month! Full steam ahead!

**March 2026:**

I had the opportunity to attend the first (and hopefully not the last!) iteration of EUCLIDYAN (European Climate Dynamics Action Network) in Ringberg, southern Germany. This included three days of fun and science in the foothills of the German Alps. As part of the workshop, I participated in a small-group project led by Moritz Günther of MPI, investigating the question: Can we understand the CMIP6 intermodel spread of the Eastern Pacific response from land–sea contrasts?

It was great fun working with a variety of people on the project and getting to approach the problem from different perspectives. The St Andrews group that attended will be carrying the torch for next year's meeting, where we are excited to host folks in the foothills of the Scottish Highlands!

Read more about it [HERE](https://mpimet.mpg.de/en/communication/news/connected-across-europe-the-euclidyan-initiative-on-climate-dynamics)

**December 2025:**

I had the opportunity to present at the [Scottish Climate Science Network Meeting](https://www.instagram.com/p/DSKrw9PDDUt/) hosted at St Andrews. I presented work that we are currently preparing for publication entitled: *"Climate Models Underestimate Near-Surface Drying over Tropical Land"*. It was really great to hear from people from all over Scotland and made me relize the variety and depth of climate science expertise that is right here in Scotland.

**September 2025:**

I attended a [climate modeling summer school hosted by NCAS](https://ncas.ac.uk/level-up-your-expertise-with-our-climate-modelling-summer-school/) at the university of Cambridge for the past two weeks! We had the opportunity to hear and learn from experts in a variety of different feilds. The school culminated in group projects where we worked with [SpeedyWeather](https://github.com/SpeedyWeather/SpeedyWeather.jl) (a light weight climate model) and presented our interesting (and hastily put together) findings. 

We additionally had our first CDL group away day hosted at the SOI in St Andrews. It was great to hear about the groups future directions and how everyones work contributes to the larger project of understanding controls on extreme temperatures in a changing climate.

I passed my first year annual review! I'll spend the first part of my PhD developing an emergent constraint for extreme temperatures (in the tropics) and leveraging theoretical understanding around what controls surface moist static energy changes over land in the tropics.
